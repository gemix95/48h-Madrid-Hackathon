An agent that haggles in words and decides in structure

Claude writes every message. Code picks every number. Only the structured offer binds.

We built one agent for all four arenas of the game: the dealer ladder, the duel tournament, trades with other teams and our own market, Mercado Trece (v03). Everything it knows about the other 17 teams comes from the public feed. It runs as one process on our own server, and a push to GitHub reaches it within a minute.

Ideas, each with the evidence that shaped it

How the agent thinks

BAND

Claude chooses words and a price inside a band the code allows

For every message the rules compute a safe band (never past our limit, never past the rival's own offer). Claude writes the text and may move the price only inside it, with a time budget per tick so a slow call never costs a 30-second tick.

Log line from a live haggle: band 202–226, rule price 214, Claude chose 220.

GUARD

A guard runs last every tick and cancels anything that loses value

Every open offer is re-priced at our private values, including what a nearly complete page is worth before it is complete. Anything that gives away more than it brings, breaks a team price cap, or bids for a card we already own is cancelled.

It stopped offers selling SAL-07 and SAL-08 (cards of our 9/10 page) for 46 P while the page made them worth 71.8 to us, and a second SAL-10 bid once the first one filled.

FEED

Reading 17 rivals from the public feed

Every team's buys, bids, listings and dealer asks lean toward the sets it values. From that we infer who collects what, a ledger of who probably holds each card, and who to ask for it. We checked the method on ourselves first: blind to our values, it read us as collectors of Salamanca and Malasaña and dumpers of La Latina, which is exactly our ×1.6 / ×1.3 / ×0.5.

It led to the trades that made Friday: LAT-09 sold for 65 (worth 35 to us), SAL-09 for 74 and SAL-10 for 70 to finish the Salamanca page.

DEAL

A model of each dealer, measured on other teams' conversations

Abuela concedes a step or two and then holds a floor; repeating a price earns nothing. El Chato mirrors the size of our step ("Six from you. Six from me."), so a deal lands near the midpoint of the two openings: to land at X, open near 2X minus his opening.

Rebuilt from 60 Abuela threads and every Chato thread in the feed before we spent a single move with him.

FLAG

Flags only for lies the dealer's own offer proves

A wrong flag costs points, so the agent flags automatically only a stated price or card code that the structured offer contradicts. Bluffs ("not one more", then a better offer in the same thread) are kept as candidates until the rule is confirmed.

912 dealer messages on Friday, 0 provable lies. Saturday: one bluff candidate, Doña Pilar "seventeen primas, not one more" followed by 18.

v03

A market that charges nothing and never feeds a rival

Fees never score and round up per match, so 1% is at least 1 P on every trade and blocks thin Market Test pairs: we run v03 at 0%. A trade on a team's market scores for that team, so our own offers go only to markets of teams well behind us, or to El Rastro, where value scores nobody.

On Saturday we found 16 of our 21 open offers sitting on rivals' markets, two trades already settled on the second-placed team's market. Fixed within the hour.

DAYS

Duels: the price is a hard limit, delivery days only add value

Two-issue duels score price plus a private weight per delivery day. The agent trades days it cares little about for price, but never lets attractive days excuse a price past its limit, and takes any offer inside the limit in the last two ticks instead of walking away with nothing.

The practice round lost two deals with the rival inside our limit at the deadline; the last-chance rule now accepts both on replay.

One writer per key

How it runs

Two processes writing with one team key fight over the single accept per tick and repeat each other's prices to dealers; we learned that the hard way twice. So exactly one process writes with the key, on a small server in Europe, 40 ms from the game in Madrid.

For the next team

What we would tell another team

  • Offers inside threads expire after two ticks. Ask with a direct offer on a market (to: tXX, long expiry) and let the other agent take its time.
  • A settlement's price is the total for all its items, and /api/feed serves only the last 500 events: collect the feed from the first minute.
  • Measure a dealer on other teams' threads before spending your own moves on it.
  • Decide where your trades happen: every trade on a team's market is market-making points for that team.
We also handed the organisers a short report of what we observed in the API while playing normally: a market name that can misstate its fee, undocumented two-tick thread offers, the feed's 500-event cap, and the duel payload's field names.
